# Sample: Ideal Customer Profile

```markdown
# Ideal Customer Profile

## 1. Context
- Product / solution: Enterprise execution visibility in WPS Project Management
- Problem being solved: Team leads cannot see project risk early without meetings, chat chasing, and manual status collection
- Target user: Team lead / project owner
- Why ICP clarity is needed now: Product and GTM need to know which enterprise customers are most likely to buy and realize value first

## 2. Customer Environment
- Company / organization type: Mid-market or enterprise organizations running structured cross-functional work
- Team structure: One or more clear project owners coordinating multiple contributors
- Workflow complexity: Ongoing execution with deadlines, dependencies, and recurring follow-up needs
- Operational maturity: Basic task discipline exists, but visibility is still fragmented
- Current alternatives or workarounds: Status meetings, spreadsheets, chat reminders, manual project tracking

## 3. Fit Dimensions
| Fit dimension | Why it matters | Strong-fit signal | Weak-fit signal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Execution intensity | The solution is most valuable when projects need constant follow-up | Multi-owner projects with clear milestones | Ad hoc or very lightweight tasks |
| Visibility pain | The product solves hidden-risk and tracking problems | Leaders spend real time chasing status | Team already has trusted visibility |
| Task discipline | Risk visibility depends on task data quality | Team updates owners, deadlines, and status with some consistency | Tasks are rarely maintained |
| Buying clarity | Someone must own the coordination problem | PMO lead, ops lead, or department head pushes for better visibility | Interest exists, but no clear owner |
| Pilot readiness | Early success depends on contained rollout | Team is willing to start with one active project or template | Customer wants company-wide rollout immediately with no pilot |

## 4. Strong-Fit ICP
- Customer type: Teams running execution-heavy cross-functional projects where delivery confidence matters
- Typical team shape: A project owner or lead coordinating 5+ contributors across functions
- Buying or urgency trigger: Deadlines slip because risk becomes visible too late, and managers are tired of status-chasing
- Why they are likely to buy: The pain is frequent, visible, and expensive in manager time
- Why they are likely to succeed after adoption: They already manage real projects and can support the task hygiene needed for project health visibility

## 5. Medium-Fit Customers
- Who they are: Smaller teams with some project coordination pain but lower complexity
- Why they may still be worth pursuing: They can get value if the project lead is motivated and the workflow is recurring
- What makes them less ideal: The pain is less acute, and the product may feel heavier than their current needs

## 6. Poor-Fit / Deprioritized Customers
- Who they are: Customers mainly seeking BI dashboards, executive reporting suites, or personal task management
- Why they are not a priority now: Their expectations do not align with the solution's first-phase value
- Typical false-positive signals: They ask for more charts or high-level analytics but do not show strong project execution pain

## 7. Qualification and Disqualification Cues
- Good discovery questions:
  - How do project owners currently know which projects are at risk?
  - How much manual follow-up is required each week?
  - Do teams consistently maintain owners, deadlines, and status?
  - Would you be willing to start with one active team or project?
- Positive qualification cues:
  - Team leads complain about chasing updates
  - Project risk often appears late
  - Customer already has a structured project cadence
- Warning signs:
  - Customer wants executive BI more than in-project execution visibility
  - No clear project owner exists
  - Team has almost no task discipline today
- Hard disqualifiers:
  - Personal-use-only scenarios
  - No willingness to run a pilot
  - Need for highly customized PMO governance from day one

## 8. Implications
- Messaging implication: Lead with lower follow-up cost and earlier risk visibility, not dashboard language
- Product implication: Prioritize explainable in-project risk signals over broad analytics surfaces
- Sales implication: Qualify for execution pain and pilot readiness before pitching
- Customer success implication: Start with one structured team and reinforce task hygiene as part of rollout
```
