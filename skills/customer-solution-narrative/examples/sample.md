# Sample: Customer Solution Narrative

```markdown
# Customer Solution Narrative

## 1. Solution Context
- Solution name: Enterprise Execution Visibility
- Product / capability: Project health summary and risk follow-up inside WPS Project Management
- Customer segment: Mid-market and enterprise teams managing cross-functional execution
- Primary buyer / influencer: Department head, PMO lead, operations lead
- Primary user: Team lead or project owner
- Trigger scenario: Teams are running active projects, but leaders only learn about delays during manual status collection

## 2. Customer Problem
- Current situation: Project owners rely on scattered updates, chat follow-ups, and manual status meetings to understand execution health
- Operational pain: Risks surface late, blocked tasks are easy to miss, and managers spend too much time chasing updates
- Business consequence: Teams react slowly, coordination overhead rises, and deadlines slip without clear early warning
- Current workaround or alternative: Weekly reporting rituals, spreadsheets, chat pings, or a separate dashboard nobody keeps updated

## 3. Why This Matters Now
- What changed: Cross-team execution has become more distributed, while managers are under pressure to improve visibility without adding more reporting overhead
- Why the old way is no longer good enough: Manual collection scales poorly and usually exposes risk after the project is already off track

## 4. Solution Story
- What the solution helps the customer do: See project health in context, identify risk tasks early, and take follow-up action before problems turn into delays
- How it fits into the customer's workflow: The project owner opens the project they already manage, reviews the health summary, drills into risk items, and follows up from the same working context
- What becomes easier, faster, safer, or more visible: Teams spend less effort on status chasing and gain earlier, clearer visibility into execution risk

## 5. Core Value Pillars
| Value pillar | Customer meaning | Product mechanism | Proof or evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Earlier risk visibility | Leaders spot slippage before the weekly review | Rule-based health summary and risk task list inside each project | Customer demand clustered around execution tracking and risk visibility |
| Lower follow-up overhead | Less manual chasing across chat and meetings | In-context drill-down and follow-up actions | Current workflow relies on fragmented updates |
| Better execution conversations | Teams discuss real blockers, not status theater | Shared visibility into blocked, overdue, and at-risk work | Aligns to enterprise collaboration scenarios |

## 6. Expected Outcomes
- Operational outcome: Faster identification of blocked or overdue work
- Management outcome: Team leads gain a more reliable view of project execution without building separate reporting rituals
- Business outcome: Better on-time delivery confidence and lower coordination overhead
- Success indicators: Risk items viewed, follow-up actions taken, reduction in manual status collection effort

## 7. Best-Fit Customers
- Strong fit: Teams running active multi-owner projects and struggling to monitor execution consistently
- Moderate fit: Smaller teams with lighter collaboration needs but some recurring coordination overhead
- Not yet a fit: Organizations seeking a heavy PMO reporting suite or highly customized enterprise governance workflows

## 8. Adoption and Rollout Story
- Recommended starting point: Launch with one or two active execution-heavy teams
- First visible value: Team leads can see overdue, blocked, or low-momentum work in one project view
- Expansion path: Extend from core project health usage into team-level execution review habits
- Customer success guidance: Start with one project template, coach project owners on interpreting risk signals, and review action quality after the first cycle

## 9. Common Objections and Responses
| Objection | Why it comes up | Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "We already have status meetings" | Teams think ritual equals visibility | This solution reduces the effort needed to prepare and improves the quality of what is discussed |
| "We do not want another dashboard" | Customers fear extra tooling overhead | The value comes from in-project visibility, not a separate reporting surface |
| "Will this create more noise?" | Managers worry about alert fatigue | The first release emphasizes visible project health and focused follow-up rather than noisy broad alerts |

## 10. Messaging Guardrails
- Do say: In-context execution visibility for project owners and team leads
- Do not say: Full executive BI platform or autonomous AI project management
- Non-goals or boundaries: This is not positioned as a replacement for enterprise-wide reporting suites in the first phase
```
